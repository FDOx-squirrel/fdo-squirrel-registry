The Govan Sarcophagus (Govan 1, Govan Stones)

3DDataFDO · catalogue record 24346314

3D model of the Govan Sarcophagus (Govan 1), the best-known of the Govan Stones and a rare monument type in early medieval Scotland before the 12th century. Carved from solid sandstone, it is decorated with a hunting scene, differently arranged interlaced beasts and six panels of interlace, some of which appear to be interlaced snakes. It was rediscovered in the churchyard of Govan Old Parish Church in the mid-1850s and is now displayed in the Govan Stones museum there; how its lid looked is not known. The model was recorded by structure-from-motion photogrammetry for Megan N. Kasten's PhD research on the Govan Stones (University of Glasgow, 2019) and was published on Sketchfab by the Govan Stones account under CC BY 4.0.

Early medieval sarcophagus from Govan Old (Govan Old Parish Church, Govan Road, Glasgow), rediscovered in the churchyard by a gravedigger in the mid-1850s (sources give 1855 and 1856); now displayed in the Govan Stones museum in the church. Numbered Govan 1 in the collection. The only sarcophagus of its kind recorded from pre-Norman northern Britain.
